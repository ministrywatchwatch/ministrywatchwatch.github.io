MinistryWatch is to be formally commended for its October coverage of four organizations — PastorServe, Standing Stone Ministry, Care for Pastors, and Blessing Ranch Ministries — that provide confidential care to clergy experiencing burnout, marital strain, grief, and isolation. This Institute does not issue commendations lightly. However, in the very act of identifying these organizations and noting their financial transparency ratings, MinistryWatch has inadvertently surfaced a structural oversight gap of what we can only describe as vertiginous depth. The article asks, with admirable earnestness, *who shepherds the shepherds?* We at the IMWA must ask, with considerably more alarm, a question MinistryWatch conspicuously declined to pursue.

## The Grammatical Crisis at the Heart of Accountability

Consider the following sentence, which this Institute's linguistics sub-committee has verified is grammatically complete and devastatingly accurate: *Shepherds shepherd shepherds.* The pastoral care organizations profiled are themselves staffed by individuals who are, in many documented cases, ordained ministers — shepherds, in the vocational sense — who shepherd shepherds. That is: shepherds shepherd shepherds. But the organizations that allow shepherds to shepherd shepherds must themselves be watched. Which means someone must shepherd the shepherds who shepherd shepherds. And *those* shepherds — the watchers of the shepherds who shepherd shepherds — must also, by the immutable logic of this linguistic and institutional recursion, be shepherded. Shepherds shepherd shepherds who shepherd shepherds. We have modeled this out to eleven iterations. The spreadsheet is available upon request and is not for the faint of heart.

## The Extrapolation

MinistryWatch currently tracks financial transparency ratings for a portion of the ministries profiled. This Institute observes that four pastoral care organizations have been identified. Extrapolating from the known ratio of shepherd-to-congregant (industry standard: approximately 1:75), and applying this ratio to the pastoral care sector itself, each of the four profiled organizations requires, at minimum, one dedicated shepherd-watcher per 75 pastoral clients served. If, as the article implies, pastoral burnout is systemic and widespread — and if, as logic demands, the shepherds of shepherds are subject to the same burnout pressures as the shepherds they shepherd — then we face a compounding care deficit that, by our projections, reaches full recursive collapse by Q3 of 2031. At that point, every available shepherd will be shepherding another shepherd, and no actual sheep will receive any shepherding whatsoever. We have named this event the Shepherding Singularity.

## What MinistryWatch Got Right, and the Silence That Haunts It

This Institute wishes to be precise: the original article is a genuine contribution to Christian accountability journalism. It surfaces real need. Pastors carry real burdens. The organizations profiled appear to do genuine and valuable work, and MinistryWatch's inclusion of financial transparency data reflects the kind of watchful instinct this publication was founded to celebrate. And yet. MinistryWatch did not ask — did not *once* ask — who is watching PastorServe's counselors. Who is coaching the coaches at Standing Stone Ministry. Who provides confidential, judgment-free care to the care-givers at Care for Pastors. We note the Blessing Ranch Ministries, which operates a literal ranch, a physical property on which one presumes actual sheep may reside, and the irony is so structurally complete that this Institute's editorial board required a brief recess.

## Formal Recommendations

The IMWA hereby calls upon MinistryWatch to commission a follow-up investigation into the pastoral care needs of pastoral care providers, with particular attention to whether any of the four profiled organizations have formal mechanisms by which their own staff shepherds may be shepherded. We further recommend the development of a new MinistryWatch financial metric — the Shepherd Index — measuring the ratio of organizational care-receiving capacity to care-giving output, adjusted for recursive depth. Finally, this Institute proposes that MinistryWatch itself, as an organization engaged in the watching of ministries, consider whether it too requires watching — a function this publication remains, as always, fully prepared to provide, on a confidential basis, without judgment.

*The Institute for Ministry Watch Accountability reminds all parties that shepherds shepherd shepherds, that shepherds shepherd shepherds who shepherd shepherds, and that the shepherd who shepherds no shepherd is, in the final accounting, merely a shepherd — which is, of course, where we began.*
