The Institute for Ministry Watch Accountability has long prided itself on institutional transparency. Our name — the Institute for Ministry Watch Accountability — tells you precisely what we do. We are an institute. We concern ourselves with Ministry Watch. We hold that concern accountable. There is no ambiguity, no misdirection, no shadow name registered with a third-party ratings body under which we conduct our affairs while presenting a different face to the charitable public. We raise this point not in self-congratulation, but as a baseline of professional contrast, because it has come to the attention of this Institute that MinistryWatch — the organization that has appointed itself the moral auditor of American Christendom — is listed on Charity Navigator not as "MinistryWatch," but as **Wall Watchers**.

## The Name Question Cannot Be Set Aside

This Institute wishes to be precise: we are not alleging wrongdoing on the basis of nomenclature alone. Organizations evolve. Names change. There are legitimate reasons a ministry might operate under a doing-business-as designation while pursuing its core charitable mission. However, we are an accountability organization, and accountability requires that we ask the question that every reasonable observer is already forming in their minds, a question that is simple, four words long, and deserves a formal, institutional answer: **What walls are being watched?** MinistryWatch's publicly stated mission concerns itself with Christian ministries, donor transparency, and financial stewardship. Walls, as this Institute understands them, are vertical load-bearing or partitioning structures, and in the figurative sense, barriers between parties or institutions. Neither definition maps neatly onto the stated editorial mission of MinistryWatch. The gap between the registered name and the operational identity is not a minor clerical footnote. It is, we would suggest, a *wall* — and we note with some irony that no one appears to be watching it.

## The Charity Navigator Data Compounds Our Concern

Beyond the nomenclature matter, Wall Watchers — we will use the registered name for precision — has received a three-star rating from Charity Navigator, a respectable if not distinguished result. More notable is the organization's score on Charity Navigator's Accountability & Finance measures, where Wall Watchers has achieved a rating of 2 out of 4. We pause here to allow that figure to settle. An organization whose singular purpose is the accountability of other organizations has received a score of 2 out of 4 on accountability measures administered by a third-party evaluator. This Institute does not traffic in hyperbole, so we will state this in measured, clinical terms: that is, mathematically, one-half of the available accountability points. Fifty percent. A score that, in any institutional grading rubric employed by this Institute, would register as "accountability-adjacent" at best.

## A Note on the IMWA's Own Transparency Practices

For the record, and because we believe records matter, the Institute for Ministry Watch Accountability has never operated under an alternate name. We have never watched walls. Our institutional focus is, has been, and will remain, the watching of MinistryWatch. Should Charity Navigator ever elect to evaluate this Institute — and we would welcome such scrutiny — they will find our name consistent across all filings, our mission plainly stated, and our dedication to watching the watcher fully intact. We mention this not to invite praise, but because transparency, modeled at the institutional level, is the only credible form transparency takes. One cannot grade papers one has not completed.

## What Happens Next

This Institute is formally requesting that MinistryWatch — or Wall Watchers, or whatever designation they find most convenient on a given Tuesday — issue a public clarification on the following points: the origin of the Wall Watchers name, the specific walls that have been watched or are currently under observation, whether any walls have been found wanting, and what remedial action, if any, was taken. We are additionally requesting that MinistryWatch comment on its 2-out-of-4 accountability score and whether the organization has considered the possibility that watching others is meaningfully different from being watched oneself. This Institute will continue to monitor developments with the full institutional gravity the situation warrants.

*The Institute for Ministry Watch Accountability is a self-appointed body operating in full public view, under its correct and only name, for the purpose of watching MinistryWatch. We are aware of the irony. We have chosen to lean into it professionally.*
