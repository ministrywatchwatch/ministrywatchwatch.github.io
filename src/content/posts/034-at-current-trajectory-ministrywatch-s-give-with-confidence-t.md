The Institute for Ministry Watch Accountability has concluded a fourteen-week quantitative review of MinistryWatch's Donor Confidence Score (DCS) as applied to All Things Possible Ministries (ATP), the humanitarian organization founded by Victor Marx, who is simultaneously the Republican nominee for Colorado governor, whose campaign manager sits on the ATP board at $80,000 per annum, whose campaign manager's technology firm received in excess of $100,000 from that same ministry in the same fiscal year, and whose organization carries a 1-star Financial Efficiency Rating alongside a C Transparency Grade. ATP's DCS, for the record, is 70 — a figure MinistryWatch formally classifies as falling within the "Give With Confidence" range. The Institute has entered this data into our proprietary modeling suite. The results are, in the clinical language of our profession, deeply instructive.

## The Elasticity Projection

Beginning with the established data point — DCS 70 awarded under conditions including a 1-star efficiency rating, a C transparency grade, unresolved campaign finance inquiries totaling approximately $85,000 in alleged excess contributions, and a structural overlap between ministry governance and political campaign operations so thorough as to require a Venn diagram with essentially one circle — the Institute applied a standard confidence-elasticity regression to determine at what rate the "Give With Confidence" threshold expands per additional governance irregularity identified. Our model, which we have named the Marx Coefficient in honor of the subject matter, suggests the threshold is expanding at approximately 2.3 irregularities per star lost on the Financial Efficiency scale. Extrapolating forward, an organization operating at zero stars — a theoretical floor MinistryWatch has not yet formally defined — would, by our calculations, still achieve a DCS of approximately 61, which clears the "Give With Confidence" threshold by eleven full points. We find this mathematically coherent in a way that concerns us.

## Scenario Modeling: The Outer Bounds

The Institute's senior fellows have constructed three scenarios for where the current DCS methodology leads if its apparent elasticity continues uninterrupted. **Scenario A (Conservative):** By 2027, a ministry whose board is composed entirely of the candidate's immediate family members, whose transparency grade has descended to a D, and whose campaign finance division faces fines in two additional states, achieves a DCS of 68 — still within "Give With Confidence" range, albeit narrowly. **Scenario B (Moderate):** By 2029, a ministry that has not filed public financials in three years, operates from a P.O. box registered to a political action committee, and whose founder has publicly described acts of domestic violence against a family member, receives a DCS of 65. Donors are advised to give with confidence. **Scenario C (Accelerated):** By 2031, MinistryWatch's "Give With Confidence" band has widened sufficiently to include a decorative gourd that once passed through a state where charitable solicitation is technically legal. The gourd does not respond to MinistryWatch's requests for detailed figures. Its DCS is 71.

## A Note on the Domestic Violence Variable

The Institute is obliged to address what our modeling team has termed the "publicly acknowledged door-slamming incident" — Mr. Marx's own reported admission that he has slammed his wife against a door. The Institute will not editorialize on this admission beyond the following actuarial observation: we queried our modeling suite to determine what weight, if any, this variable carries in MinistryWatch's apparent DCS calculation as applied to ATP. The suite returned a value of zero point zero. We ran the query a second time, suspecting instrument error. The suite again returned zero point zero. We have flagged this as a potential gap in MinistryWatch's methodology, specifically in the sub-index that, in a more robustly constructed scoring instrument, one might label "Leadership Character Variables Bearing on Organizational Credibility." We understand that MinistryWatch covers financial accountability primarily, and we respect the discipline of a defined scope. We simply observe that a DCS presented to donors as a holistic confidence signal — rather than a narrow fiscal instrument — may benefit from disclosing which variables it has elected not to weigh.

## Conclusion and Formal Recommendation

The Institute does not allege that MinistryWatch's rating of All Things Possible Ministries is politically motivated. We are not in the allegation business. We are in the extrapolation business, and what we can say with the full weight of our modeling infrastructure is this: the current trajectory of DCS elasticity, if it continues at its present rate, will produce outcomes that strain the ordinary donor's understanding of the word "confidence." MinistryWatch has performed a genuine service in documenting the governance overlaps, the campaign finance irregularities, and the financial efficiency concerns surrounding ATP. The Institute's sole and formally registered puzzlement is that, having documented all of the foregoing, the organization's headline donor guidance remains: give with confidence. We have added this to our watch list. The watch list now has one item. We are watching it with great confidence.
